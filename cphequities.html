A FEW THINGS TO UNDERSTAND ABOUT THE AI SELL-OFF 💡 The last few weeks have been rough for anyone invested in AI stocks. NVIDIA - which I am not invested in - is almost 40% below its October high, and Micron and TSMC have dropped 24% and 16% in a month. This is obviously not fun to watch, but stocks cannot go up uninterrupted, and to be honest I prefer this to a meltup with stocks flying up, followed by a bubble bursting. For now, this is much more controlled, although the $SPX500 (SPX500 Index (Non Expiry)) has now entered correction territory and we have had a fair number of red days recently. We have to remember, though, that this follows three years of double-digit returns in the market. There are real reasons for the nervousness in the market: - Interest rates are rising. The Fed hiked rates in September for the first time since 2023, and the 10-year yield is above 5%. A fair share of the AI buildout is now financed with debt, and the weakest borrowers feel it first. CoreWeave's unsecured bonds yield around 16%. This is something to watch, and I take it seriously. - Democrats won the midterms. Washington matters less than the headlines suggest, because Trump still has his veto. The real effect is local. Texas elected a governor who campaigned pausing for new data centers, and Texas alone accounts for roughly a quarter of the capacity planned in the US. - Jensen Huang left Nvidia. He resigned last week with immediate effect, and the company has no permanent CEO. Nobody knows who will run the most important company in AI a year from now. So the buildout can be delayed. Some data centers will be built later, in other places, and with more expensive financing, but a delay is not a lack of demand.
